Preserving the past for the future

The Mission

Problem

Culture is being lost throughout history. Without any records of that culture, archaeologists have to make assumptions, which may lead to misinformation.

Solution

Our solution is to make a website that archaeologists and reputable people from that culture can use (such as people who work for the Seminole Tribe of Florida). With this website, we provide culture with a permanent home, making it an easy and accessible resource for future archaeologists and the general public to learn about past cultures.

To ensure accuracy, we only utilized information from reputable sources (e.g., .org, .gov, .edu) and consulted with industry experts like Zindy, a bioarchaeologist, who confirmed the tool's utility for the field.

Our Journey

The First Step: Research & Design

After extensive R&D, we collaborated with Mr. Chris, a software engineer. The team explored various UI (User Interface) directions, including social media, Reddit, and Wikipedia styles. We ultimately chose a hybrid Story Maps and Wikipedia layout to best present cultural narratives. Mr. Chris then guided us through the fundamentals of web development.

The Second Step: Development

Once we understood the core languages of the web, we began the build. Mr. Chris helped us architect the information display. After several beta versions, we officially launched the site on the World Wide Web!

Programming Languages Used

HTML

The standard language designed for web browsers to display text and create simple interactive elements like buttons.

JavaScript

Used to make the website interactive and power advanced functionality and buttons.

CSS

Used for animations and visual styling to give the website an "extra pop" and professional feel.
